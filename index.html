Lead Gen Model

Design Research & UX Insights — RealPro Portfolio
Portfolio-Level Summary
The portfolio is moving toward a single self-serve ecosystem where agents discover products, understand value, buy with less friction, manage plans and billing, and get nudged back into action through dashboard and app surfaces. The core UX challenge is not improving any one surface — it is creating a coherent loop across surfaces. Research repeatedly points to the same portfolio-level issues: weak product awareness, unclear information architecture, insufficient confidence in pricing and value, friction in purchase and account management flows, and too many moments where users call Sales rather than continue self-serve.

Source: RealPro Portfolio Design Research Overview — synthesizing Online Store Study, RPA IA Study, Billing Exploratory Study, Dashboard research, and related briefs. Co-authored with Glean.
Four Cross-Cutting Themes
Theme 1 — All Surfaces
Product awareness is still too weak in management surfaces
"Both the dashboard and the app are being asked to do more than report performance; they are now expected to expose product value, prompt expansion, and connect users back into self-serve purchase or management flows."
  • Creates a core design tension: surfaces must stay useful for day-to-day management while also becoming better merchandising and guidance surfaces
  • If the portfolio over-rotates into promo without better relevance and timing, it risks reducing trust rather than increasing conversion
Theme 2 — Online Store + Dashboard
Confidence is a bigger blocker than usability
"Users often hesitate not because they cannot click through, but because they do not trust what they are seeing enough to buy. When information is incomplete, many default to calling Sales."
  • Agents want clarity on: exclusivity, lead routing mechanics, ROI, available volume, and exactly what a product includes
  • Dashboard plan management, billing visibility, and product education are all part of the same trust problem — not separate issues
  • If agents don't understand what they have, what they pay for, and what outcome to expect, self-serve growth stays fragile
Theme 3 — Architecture
Information architecture is a portfolio issue, not a page issue
"The portfolio still lacks a stable mental model for users."
  • Dashboard research: unclear navigation and information hierarchy
  • App: missing product-management paths
  • Store: missed recommendations, confusing labels, weak upfront framing
  • Billing: simultaneously being carved into its own management center
  • Strategic design question: where should the line sit between discovering/buying, understanding plan/value, managing billing/settings, and getting nudged toward next best action?
Theme 4 — Cross-Surface
Cross-surface entry points are a major design lever
"Design quality at the seams matters as much as design quality inside a given flow."
  • Online Store strategy explicitly calls for entry and exit patterns from RPD, RPA, and other ecosystem surfaces into self-serve purchase paths
  • App is being used to surface product opportunities and route people into richer dashboard or store experiences
  • Entry trigger quality, state preservation, terminology consistency, and expectation-setting will have outsized impact
Surface-by-Surface Research Findings
RealPro Dashboard
What research found
  • Unclear navigation and information hierarchy throughout
  • Low product and plan awareness — users don't know what they own
  • Terminology comprehension issues across key product concepts
  • Limited paths that help users grow or manage effectively from within the experience
  • Need for stronger lead-performance storytelling and better visibility into plan and billing context
"The dashboard likely needs a more deliberate separation between: performance interpretation, plan/account management, billing actions, and product discovery or upsell moments."
Design Risk
Too many responsibilities layered onto one surface without a clear conceptual model. Strong IA and interaction model needed before individual feature work compounds the confusion.
RealPro App
What research found
  • Low product awareness — agents don't know what products they have through the app
  • Limited pathways for product management, growth, or broader self-serve interaction
  • App has been optimized for operational touchpoints (lead alerts, status updates) but not commercial or account-growth touchpoints
"Treat the app as the portfolio's highest-signal re-entry surface, not as a compressed clone of the other products."
Design Framing
  • Which moments belong natively in mobile?
  • Which should deep-link into dashboard or store?
  • What context must travel with the user across that jump?
  • If notifications aren't well-timed, users will perceive them as marketing noise, not service
Online Store
Specific research findings (richest signal in portfolio)
  • CTA language matters: Users prefer personalized prompts like "Check Availability" over generic ones like "Get Leads"
  • Shared leads distrust: "Shared" lead routing creates distrust when actual meaning and share count aren't obvious
  • Ambiguous volume framing: "Total Available Leads" is ambiguous without timeframe context, distorting value perception
  • Incomplete info → Sales call: When pricing, exclusivity, availability, or offer details are unclear, many agents default to calling Sales
  • Recommendations are missable: Recommended markets can be valuable but are easy to miss and need stronger framing
  • Testimonials are mixed: Help some users but hurt credibility if they don't feel verifiable or locally relevant
  • Pricing behind login: Generally acceptable, but can still function as a barrier if not handled carefully
"The deeper problem is confidence architecture: helping agents feel they understand the product, the market, the expected return, and the tradeoffs well enough to self-serve."
Billing / Products & Billing Center
What research found
  • Users generally understood what to expect under "Billing" — one of the few domains with a relatively stable mental model
  • But understanding one billing page ≠ understanding the whole commercial relationship
  • Users still need better comprehension of: what plan they have, what products they own, how billing connects to value, and what actions they can take themselves
"The hard problem is not 'make billing cleaner.' It is 'decide what belongs in billing versus dashboard, and make that boundary obvious to users.'"
Task Model for Billing
  • What users have
  • What they owe
  • What is changing
  • What actions are available
  • What outcomes to expect after each action
Key Design Tensions to Manage
Dashboard vs. Billing
Real boundary question between "manage my products and plan" and "manage my billing and account." Both are being improved, but the user should not have to reverse-engineer the system's internal ownership model to know where to go.
Store vs. Sales
The store wants to grow self-serve revenue, but research shows users still reach for Sales when information is incomplete or trust is low. The portfolio needs a clearer philosophy for when to help users continue self-serve and when to intentionally off-ramp to assisted selling without losing context. Goal should be "lower unnecessary reliance on Sales" — not "remove Sales from the journey."
App vs. Dashboard
The app is a prompt and re-entry mechanism; dashboard is the deeper management surface. Design challenge: prevent duplicated, partial, or inconsistent experiences across the two. Context must travel with the user when they cross surfaces.
Merchandising vs. Trust
The portfolio wants more cross-sell, upsell, personalization, and growth loops. But research shows users are highly sensitive to ambiguity in value claims, lead quality, routing, and ROI. If merchandising gets ahead of clarity, trust will erode.
Portfolio Design Pressure-Test Questions
01
Is there a shared portfolio IA?
Does the team have an explicit cross-surface model for: discover → evaluate → buy → manage → pay → optimize? If not, feature work is likely outrunning the conceptual structure.
02
Is there a single confidence framework?
What information does the team believe users need before they will: buy in store, change plans in dashboard, trust a billing action, or act on a mobile notification? Research suggests these are not separate problems — they share a common trust model.
03
Are cross-surface seams designed intentionally?
How are entry points, deep links, and return paths currently defined between app, dashboard, store, and billing? Does the handoff preserve user context — state, terminology, and expectations?
04
Is the team clear on where assisted selling should remain?
Is the goal to eliminate sales handoffs or to improve them? Some users genuinely want reassurance and context. The right framing is "lower unnecessary reliance on Sales" — not "remove Sales from the journey entirely."

Market Types — How RDC Classifies Lead Markets
Pure Market
A geographic market where RDC operates a full referral model. Consumers who submit inquiries are screened by CSRs and dispatched exclusively to a matched agent via live transfer. No shared leads — each referral goes to one agent.
Products Available
  • ReadyConnect Concierge (RCC) — commission share at close
  • Market VIP (MVIP) — upfront SOV payment, exclusive referrals
Payment Model
  • RCC: Commission share fee (pay at close)
  • MVIP: Upfront Share of Voice (SOV)
Why It Exists
  • Gives RDC highest margin model — paid on close, not upfront slot
  • Requires sufficient consumer inquiry volume to operate profitably
  • Markets with enough volume get converted from Choice → Pure
Choice Market
A geographic market where RDC operates a lead generation model. Consumer inquiries are delivered directly to agents without CSR screening. Agents subscribe to market slots and receive leads tied to specific listings.
Products Available
  • Connections Plus Flex — non-shared, sent to 1 C+ agent
  • Connections Plus Fast — shared, sent to 2 C+ agents simultaneously
Payment Model
  • Upfront slot(s) — monthly subscription per market
Why It Exists
  • Lower consumer volume markets where referral model isn't viable
  • Lower barrier to entry for agents — no commission share required
  • ReadyConnect Concierge is also available in Choice markets as a fallback
Mixed / All Markets
Some products are available across both Pure and Choice markets. ReadyConnect Concierge (RCC) and ReadyConnect NextGen Leads operate in all markets. The long-term strategic question for Core (C+) clients in mixed-market design is still being defined.
Products Available in All Markets
  • ReadyConnect Concierge — qualified referrals, commission share
  • ReadyConnect NextGen Leads — unqualified lead inquiries, upfront slots
  • LEX, SPL, Listing Toolkit — branding/promotion not market-type dependent
FY27 Open Question
  • "Define long-term strategy for Core in mixed-market design" — explicitly listed as a roadmap item; no resolution yet
  • Project Apex: tier-aware CPL routing and MVIP coexistence across market types
  • Scale Unity (R2P2): managing MVIP + C+ as combined shared inventory
How Market Conversion Works
Markets start as Choice (lead gen model). As consumer inquiry volume grows, RDC may convert a market to Pure (referral model), enabling MVIP and RCC. The E2E inventory management initiative (Scale Unity / R2P2) is building the SSOT tools and automation that make market conversion, rationalization, and combined MVIP+C+ inventory management operationally scalable. Revenue targets tied to Unity conversion: $14.5M ACV each in Q3 and Q4 FY27.
