Starting Point
This is one problem with four expressions
Before going surface by surface, there is a more useful frame. The dashboard, app, online store, and the Activate and Engage capabilities are not four separate design problems assigned to four designers. They are four expressions of a single underlying problem: agents do not have a coherent picture of what they own, what it is doing for them, and what they should do next. Every surface is trying to solve a piece of that. The design opportunity — and it is a real one — is to hold the thread that connects them, so that agents experience a coherent platform rather than a collection of well-executed surfaces.
The Through-Line
The NPS is -19. Thirty-one percent churn before year two. Less than 1% multi-product self-serve. These are not feature failures. They are failures of coherence — agents never built a strong enough mental model of the platform to feel invested in it. FY27 is the year the platform has a real shot at changing that, because for the first time it is moving from fragmented portals into a unified experience. The design team's job is to make that unification feel real to the agent, not just architectural.
The Coherence Loop — What Agents Need Across the Platform
Step 1 — Activate
Arrive and understand what they have
  • Know what products they own, what they're paying, what's active
  • Profile and settings ready without friction
  • Context-appropriate first value: a team lead and a solo agent arrive to different things
Step 2 — Engage
Understand how it's performing for them
  • Lead performance is legible, not just visible
  • Benchmarks show them where they stand vs. peers
  • Real Pro Assist surfaces the "so what" — what to change, not just what happened
Step 3 — Grow
Know what to do next, and do it themselves
  • Product discovery and purchase happen without a sales call
  • Plans, billing, and upgrades are self-manageable
  • Store entry points feel like natural next steps, not ads
The Design Gap That Cuts Across All Three
Research is consistent across surfaces: product awareness is lower than it should be — agents aren't always clear on what they own or what it's doing for them. This shows up in RPD, RPA, and the store as three expressions of the same underlying IA gap. A coordinated answer across all three — shared terminology, consistent mental model, clear paths between surfaces — is worth more than any individual surface solving it alone.

The app is the portfolio's highest-signal re-entry surface. The data is clear: 85% of activated agents won a lead. 91% received lead alerts. Activation on mobile drives performance. The design challenge is not making the app do everything — it is being deliberate about which moments belong natively in mobile, and which should be a deep link into dashboard or store.
Your Strategic Frame for RPA
The app has been optimized for operational touchpoints — lead alerts, status updates — but not for commercial or account-growth touchpoints. Will's work this year is the first time the app gets a serious Activate layer. The design frame that matters: the app is not a compressed clone of the dashboard. It is the surface where agents re-engage when they've been away, respond when they're in the field, and get nudged toward next best actions at high-signal moments. Every feature decision should pass the test: does this belong in a moment of motion, or a moment of reflection?
The Right Questions for RPA Design Decisions
Belongs natively in mobile
  • Lead alerts and response — 5 seconds matters
  • Status updates when an agent is in the field
  • Quick performance check — "how am I doing this week"
  • AI nudges timed to agent behavior (not platform business goals)
  • Training content surfaced when performance gap is detected
  • Profile completion steps that are short and actionable
Should deep-link to Dashboard or Store
  • Full lead management and filtering
  • Plan changes, billing, or product purchases
  • Complex performance analysis or benchmark review
  • CRM integration setup
  • Roster management and team hierarchy
  • Multi-step product discovery flows

Online Store — Grow
The store is the most measurably successful design surface in the portfolio right now. The PDP redesign drove +19% conversion. The 1-page checkout drove +7pp. The design work is documented, attributable, and directly tied to revenue. The FY27 target is $22M — a 16% increase on the $19M run rate. The roadmap to get there is design-heavy: catalog, comparison, social proof, AI recommendations, entry points from RPD and RPA.
Your Strategic Frame for the Store
The store's core design problem is not usability — agents can click through. It is confidence architecture. Research names it precisely: agents hesitate because they don't trust what they're seeing enough to buy. They want to know: what exactly does this include, what is the ROI, how exclusive is the lead, how many other agents are in my market. When those answers are incomplete, they call Sales. The design goal is not to remove Sales from the journey — it is to answer the questions that currently drive unnecessary handoffs, so Sales can focus on complex decisions that actually need them.
Notifications vs. Tasks — A Holistic Exploration
Strategic Design Work — Lauren
Alongside the store roadmap, Lauren is exploring a question that cuts across all three surfaces: when should the platform use a notification versus a task? These are genuinely different UX patterns with different implications — notifications are ambient and asynchronous, tasks are action-oriented and persistent — but they are currently being shipped feature by feature without a governing framework. Getting clarity on this distinction early shapes how agents understand what the platform is asking of them and whether it feels like a helpful partner or a noisy dashboard. The output could be a framework, a set of design principles, or a taxonomy — what matters is that it exists before the H2 AI recommendations and push notification strategy land and multiply the surface area of the problem.
Confidence Question 1
What exactly does this include?
  • Product detail pages must be specific, not summary
  • CPL model arriving in H2 — the store will need new patterns for variable pricing that don't create more confusion than they solve
  • "Life of Listing" ships July — the store's SPL PDP needs to reflect this change before it goes live

What is available in my market?
  • Market availability must be surfaced early — before an agent invests time evaluating a product that isn't available to them
  • Guest Pricing and Checkout (Q2) opens the store to unauthenticated users — availability UX without login is a new design challenge
  • "Check Availability" outperforms "Get Leads" in research — specific, actionable CTAs matter here
Confidence Question 3
What ROI should I expect?
  • The platform now has benchmarking data — this belongs in the store's value story
  • "Clients Like You" social proof (Q3) directly addresses this — design it to feel real and verifiable, not testimonial-generic
  • Conversion rates and lead quality data that already exists in RPD should flow into store persuasion where appropriate
Confidence Question 4
How does sharing/exclusivity work?
  • Research flags "shared" lead routing as a trust issue when actual meaning and share count aren't obvious
  • CPL model introduces new exclusivity options (14-day, 30-day) — these need clear explanation patterns that don't create anxiety
  • Ambiguous volume framing ("Total Available Leads") must be fixed before CPL lands — variable pricing makes this worse, not better
Confidence Question 5
Is this the right product for me?
  • Recommended markets are currently easy to miss and need stronger framing
  • AI product-market recommendation engine (Q4) addresses this systematically — design owns how recommendations are framed, not just whether they appear
  • Pre-built carts for Sales team (Q3) — design needs to ensure these feel personalized, not pre-packaged

Your job this year is to hold the thread that connects them — the coherent mental model that an agent builds across RPD, RPA, and the Store over time. Every surface decision that serves that thread is a good decision. Surface decisions that serve that thread are the ones that compound into a platform people trust. That thread is not a feature. It is not a project. It is a design position — and you are the right person to hold it.
